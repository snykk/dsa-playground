## What this area covers

This folder collects algorithms whose input is numbers and whose answer is a number, a list of numbers or a yes or no.

- **Number Theory** works on whole numbers. It finds a gcd, computes powers and inverses modulo `m`, merges congruences and finds primes. Everything is exact.
- **Advanced Math** applies the same halving idea to larger objects. Matrix exponentiation squares a matrix to reach the `k`-th term of a recurrence, and the FFT splits a sequence in two to multiply polynomials in O(n log n).

The two meet in modular exponentiation. Square and multiply for numbers is the template for matrix exponentiation, which squares matrices in the same way.

## Common mistakes

<Callout tone="warning" title="Watch out for these">
- **Overflow in 64 bits.** Modular products need 128-bit intermediates once the modulus is above about 3.04e9. Matrix entries and Fibonacci numbers overflow without a modulus.
- **Treating the FFT as exact.** It rounds from doubles, so it is exact only while coefficients stay below about 2^53.
- **Dividing modulo `m` without an inverse.** The inverse exists only when `gcd(a, m) = 1`.
- **Using a sieve for a single big number.** Use Miller-Rabin; the sieve needs O(n) memory.
</Callout>

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
  <FaqItem question="Where do I start?">
    With the Euclidean algorithm in Number Theory. Modular arithmetic, the Chinese Remainder Theorem and Miller-Rabin all depend on it directly or indirectly.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem question="How are the two sub-areas related?">
    Advanced Math reuses the halving trick of Number Theory. Square and multiply gives `a^e mod m`, and the same repeated squaring gives `A^k` for a matrix `A`.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem question="Which results are exact?">
    Everything in Number Theory is exact integer arithmetic, as is matrix exponentiation inside a 64-bit range or with a modulus. The FFT works with doubles and is exact only while coefficients stay below about 2^53.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem question="Does any of this connect to other folders?">
    Yes. Coding Theory builds a finite field on modular ideas, and Dynamic Programming uses matrix powers to speed up linear recurrences.
  </FaqItem>
</Faq>

## What this area covers

Every folder here answers one of two questions. Some answer **what problem** you are solving: sorting, searching, shortest paths, matching a pattern in a string. Others answer **how** you solve it: greedy, dynamic programming, divide and conquer, randomization, backtracking. The first kind gives you a toolbox for a known problem. The second kind gives you a method for a new one.

The areas also depend on each other:

- **Sorting and Searching** are the first algorithms to learn, and they show the divide-and-conquer idea in merge sort, quick sort and binary search.
- **Greedy** and **Dynamic Programming** are the two main ways to optimize. Greedy commits to a choice. Dynamic programming keeps every subproblem's answer.
- **Graph Algorithms** and **String Algorithms** apply those methods to graphs and to text. They use the structures from the Data_Structures folder: adjacency lists, heaps, union-find and tries.
- **Mathematical, Computational Geometry, Randomized, Coding Theory and Advanced** are specialist areas. Take them when a problem asks for them.

## A suggested path

The repository's study guide orders these areas by difficulty:

1. **Foundations.** Sorting and Searching: the O(n^2) sorts, merge sort, quick sort, linear search and binary search.
2. **Core.** Graph traversal with BFS, DFS and topological sort, as soon as you know the graph structures.
3. **Problem solving.** Greedy, then the basics of Dynamic Programming: Fibonacci, Kadane, LIS, coin change, LCS, edit distance and knapsack.
4. **Advanced.** Shortest paths, minimum spanning trees, advanced graphs, the string algorithms from pattern matching on, and advanced DP with its optimizations.
5. **Competitive extras.** Decompositions, number theory, FFT and matrix exponentiation, geometry, randomized algorithms and coding theory.

You can skip an area you already know, and the order inside the areas matters more than the order of the areas.

## Common mistakes

<Callout tone="warning" title="Watch out for these">
- **Looking for Merge Sort under Divide and Conquer.** It lives in Sorting. Divide_and_Conquer holds Strassen's matrix multiplication, and Quick Sort and Binary Search live in Sorting and Searching.
- **Treating greedy as always correct.** A greedy choice needs proof. When it fails, dynamic programming is the usual fallback.
- **Skipping the data structures.** Dijkstra needs a heap, Kruskal needs union-find and Aho-Corasick needs a trie. Learn those first.
- **Reading an area by name only.** Advanced_Algorithms holds backtracking and specialized techniques, which are not the same thing.
</Callout>

## Frequently asked questions

<Faq>
  <FaqItem question="Where should a beginner start?">
    With Sorting and Searching, after the Complexity_Analysis topics and Data_Structures/Linear. Then pick Greedy and Dynamic Programming, which is where problem solving starts.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem question="What is the difference between greedy and dynamic programming?">
    Greedy makes one best-looking choice at each step and never reconsiders it. Dynamic programming solves every subproblem once and combines the answers, so it also handles cases where a local choice is wrong.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem question="Which areas depend on data structures?">
    Most do. Graph algorithms use adjacency lists, heaps and union-find, and Aho-Corasick builds on a trie. The Data_Structures folder comes first for that reason.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem question="Where are the problem-solving patterns?">
    In the separate Problem_Patterns folder: two pointers, sliding window, prefix sums and others. They are reusable shapes of solution, while this folder holds the algorithms behind them.
  </FaqItem>
</Faq>

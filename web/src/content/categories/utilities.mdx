## What this area covers

Choosing the algorithm is only part of a contest. You also have to start from a working file, read and write fast enough, keep integers exact, and recognise problems that are not about computing a value at all. This folder collects those skills.

- **Templates** is a starting point: a boilerplate per language with fast input, math helpers and common structures.
- **Optimization Techniques** is for a solution whose idea is right but that is too slow, too big or wrong at the edge of the integer range.
- **Problem Types** is for problems defined by format: build an object, talk to a judge, or decide who wins a game.

None of these replaces an algorithm. They sit beside the Algorithms and Problem Patterns folders.

## A suggested path

1. **Copy a template** for your language, and read what it gives you: buffered input, a modular power and inverse, and a few structures.
2. **Fast I/O**, then **Precomputation**, the Beginner and first Intermediate topics of Optimization Techniques.
3. **Bitmasking** and **Modular Arithmetic Tricks**, when you meet subsets or counting modulo a prime.
4. **Constructive Algorithms** and **Interactive Problems**, once you are comfortable with greedy solutions and binary search.
5. **Game Theory**, the Advanced topic, after Bit Manipulation and dynamic programming.

## Common mistakes

<Callout tone="warning" title="Watch out for these">
- **Trusting a template blindly.** Read it first and test it on small inputs. A helper you do not understand can fail silently.
- **Optimizing before the algorithm is right.** These techniques change constants, not the order of growth.
- **Skipping the proof in a constructive problem.** A pattern that works on small cases is not yet a solution.
- **Buffering in an interactive problem.** Read and write one query at a time, and flush after every answer.
</Callout>
